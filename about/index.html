About

I build across hardware and software.

I've worked on robotic musculoskeletal simulators, rehabilitation and industrial exoskeletons, and structural-heart devices. That work taught me how to think about machines as complete systems: mechanics, sensing, controls, manufacturability, failure modes, and the people who have to live with the result.

Over time I started applying the same mindset to software. The apps I make through Fe Studio are small, opinionated tools. They're attempts to remove friction, reduce manipulation, or make a digital behavior feel more intentional.

I also write, mostly about engineering, attention, product incentives, and the strange ways software shapes what we notice and what we ignore.

What I'm doing now

I'm continuing to develop small software products, publish essays, and keep one foot in engineering work that benefits from strong systems thinking. Fe Studio is the umbrella for the apps and independent product work. This site is the more personal layer above it.

How I like to work

I'm drawn to projects that are technically rigorous but still legible to the people using them. In practice that means products with clear constraints, meaningful tradeoffs, and a chance to improve the user's environment rather than just consume more of it.
